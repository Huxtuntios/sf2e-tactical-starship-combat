# Tactical Starship Combat

## Overview
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 165*

All hands to battle stations! Your crew's starship is under heavy fire from enemy war vessels! Alarms blare, laser blasts scorch the hull, and sparks fly from consoles. Does the pilot evade enemy attacks through the asteroid field or face their foes head-on? Can the engineer repair the generator and get the shields back online before the ship takes critical damage? Does the gunner unleash a missile barrage on the foes in scanner range or leave the gunnery to repel the boarding party?

Tactical starship combat blends the round-by-round action of a typical Starfinder encounter with strategic starship-scale space battles. These encounters place the PCs aboard their very own customized starship, where they can helm battle stations to control the vessel, overcome hazards, battle enemies, and keep their starship in good working order.

Tactical starship combat works like any other combat encounter. The focus for these encounters remains on the PCs, with the map showing where they are on their starship. PCs roll initiative, take three actions per turn, move their characters on a battle map, cast spells, fire weapons, and use feats, skills, and class abilities to interact with enemies, whether they're boarding the PCs' starship or piloting their own starships within scanner range. If you want to place players at the helm of a unique vessel that levels up along with their characters and allows them to engage in tactical space battles, this subsystem is an excellent inclusion in your Starfinder campaign.

### Start Small
Most starships have a limited number of stations and station upgrades, especially at low levels, so there's no need for everyone to familiarize themselves with every single rule. It's better to start small and learn the rules that are most relevant to any given starship or player, then expand your knowledge as your party levels up and upgrades their ship.

### How These Rules Are Organized
The rules show GMs and players how to use tactical starship encounters, how players can customize their battle stations and helm them during encounters, how the action plays out, what characters can do on their turns, what happens when starships take damage, and so on.
- **Tactical Starship Rules.** Each round of a tactical starship encounter plays out much like a round of any other combat encounter, with the addition of enemy starships and space hazards represented on a sensor map. PCs move around their starship and helm battle stations to overcome these challenges.
- **Battle Stations.** The different battle stations present on most starships, such as the pilot's console, gunnery, and generator. The location of a battle station is indicated on the map you use to represent the players' starship. Using a battle station is as simple as moving to it (or beginning in its square) and using its station actions. Any character can helm any battle station.
- **Starship Equipment.** Battle stations grant a core set of actions and benefits, and players can customize their battle stations to suit their characters' needs. For example, a crew might install upgrades in a gunnery empowering the solarian to blast enemy starships with their solar flare, or select an upgrade for the magic conduit that allows a witchwarper to Warp Reality in space. The gunnery can be equipped with a wide variety of weapons. Starship expansion bays add to a starship's capabilities during exploration and downtime.
- **Advancing Starships.** As the party advances in level, so does the crew's starship. The frame determines the starship's Speed, Hull Points, Armor Class, and so on, just like a character's class determines many of their statistics. As characters level up, their starship's statistics improve based on its frame. Any two player starships might have similar default statistics based on their frame, but the selection of upgrades makes each ship unique!

## Tactical Starship Rules
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 166*

Tactical starship encounters take place in encounter mode and function just like any other combat encounter in many ways. In a tactical starship encounter, PCs and their adversaries act in sequence, using initiative and playing out a series of rounds. Each round has turns that function the same as a normal combat encounter, with each PC having the usual number of actions and reactions.

The key difference is that starship encounters take place on board the players' starship; they use a **starship map** depicting the parts of their starship as well as a **sensor map** displaying other starships and hazards in the vicinity. When acting as crew members, PCs can use most of their abilities as normal, but if they want to interact with starship-scale objects on the sensor map, they'll need to helm a battle station (such as gunnery or scanners). Station actions can only be used while helming that station, but a crew member isn't confined to a single station. A crew member might begin combat at a gunnery station, then move to the engine room to fight off boarders or fix a malfunction, then return to the gunnery station to finish off an enemy starship. Meanwhile, another crew member might spend the whole time at the pilot's console performing desperate maneuvers to evade enemy fire!

A character-scale combat encounter might take place on board the player starship—such as if a rampaging garaggakal phases through the hull and begins stalking the crew. The starship's battle stations won't have any effect against the garaggakal, and a sensor map would be useless to track its movement. To change this scene to a tactical starship encounter, the same combat might occur during a deep space skirmish with Aeon Guard starships, or the garaggakal's presence might alert a nearby Hellknight patrol sworn to purge the monster at all costs—even if it means battling the crew trying to contain it!

### What You'll Need
- A starship sheet with details about the crew's starship, its customizations, and its statistics.
- A starship map depicting the player starship, such as a Starfinder Flip-Mat, a custom map from an adventure, or a map of your own creation.
- A sensor map that's easily visible to the GM and all players.
- A list of the station actions granted by the battle stations on the player starship.
- Statistics for one or more enemy starships or starship hazards.
- Familiarity with the new rules for tactical starship combat, such as new conditions, terms, and traits.

### Starship Sheet
A starship isn't only the crew's primary mode of transportation between planets; it's also their home and refuge during long journeys. As the crew levels up, their starship grows more powerful as well. Battle stations on higher-level starships grant additional benefits and might even grant additional upgrade slots. The crew can enhance and rebuild their starship to suit their needs without any extra cost while at a port, allowing them to experiment freely with various battle stations and upgrades.

A player starship sheet has a similar structure to player character sheets. It lists the starship's Hull Points, Shield Points, AC, Speed, sensor range, size, traits, and saving throw modifiers. Many of these statistics are derived from the starship frame chosen. The starship's AC and saving throws can improve when crew members helm certain battle stations on the ship. The sheet also lists the number of expansion bays (rooms or decks that house facilities such as a medical bay or a holo den), the starship's battle stations, and other equipment like weapons and upgrades.

### Starship Map
During tactical starship combat, players represent their characters' positions and movements on board their starship using a battle map with a square grid, the same as any other combat encounter. Most tactical starship encounters occur on board the player starship, but they might also occur on board enemy starships.

Every crew should create a starship map indicating the position of each battle station. Each crew member typically begins starship combat helming a battle station, as determined by their exploration activity. Sometimes enemies board a player starship, or hazards appear on deck to represent malfunctions and other dangers. A player can spend every turn helming one battle station, or switch to another battle station during the encounter—to try a new approach against a tough enemy, because their station is malfunctioning, to step in for a teammate who goes down, or simply to change things up.

During an invasion or malfunction, the crew might choose to leave their battle stations to deal with these threats or ignore the problem until the threat comes to them. If left alone for too long, these threats can cascade into greater problems as invaders wreak havoc in an engine room or a hull breach begins to suck more and more air out of the starship's interior.

### Sensor Map
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 168*

The sensor map represents the vast region of space in which epic starship battles take place. It's separate from the map used to represent PC movement and positions on board their starship. Place the sensor map where it's visible to the GM and all players, so everyone can see both maps simultaneously. The sensor map is divided into **zones**, which are the unit of measurement for starship abilities. It is used to track the positions of starships and starship hazards during a tactical starship encounter.

## Battle Stations
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 168*

A battle station is a part of a starship that allows crew members to tackle starship-scale threats. When a creature helms a battle station, they gain access to station actions. For example, a pilot's console grants the Boost Thrusters station action, allowing the creature helming it to move the starship in the direction of its current heading. A starship's frame determines the default battle stations, the upgrades that come installed in them, and the open battle station slots available for customization. The party can change the battle stations in the customizable slots during Repair Starship downtime activities. As starships level up, battle stations gain additional slots for upgrades. The battle stations and their upgrades are recorded on the starship sheet, and their locations are marked on the starship map.

A battle station can be enhanced similar to any other equipment and gains benefits depending on its **grade**, including the ability to equip it with station upgrades. These upgrades often grant additional abilities beyond the basic functions of a battle station.

Many station actions are similar to actions a character can use in combat; for example, you can Strike using the weapons in your starship's gunnery, firing its weapons against targets within range. Many character abilities interact with battle station actions: an operative can use the Aim action to pierce cover from an asteroid and add precision damage to their gunnery Strikes against their mark, and a soldier with an automatic weapon in the gunnery can use their primary target ability to make an additional Strike when they Auto-Fire.

Most station actions require a creature to **helm** a battle station, meaning the creature is in the battle station's square, usually an apparatus or console, and is using it.

### Enemy Starships and Hazards
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 169*

The last thing you'll need for tactical starship combat—and one of the most important—is one or more enemy starships, starship hazards, or other threats. Some encounters involve creatures or hazards that appear on board the crew's starship while they're fighting enemies on the sensor map. GMs can find monsters and NPCs in Alien Core, published adventures, and other Starfinder books, or create their own using the guidelines in GM Core. Some hazards appear on the starship map rather than the sensor map and affect characters directly; many hazards presented in GM Core work well in starship combat.

## Starship Combat Structure
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 169*

Tactical starship combat is played out in a series of rounds, just like other encounters in encounter mode.
1. **Roll initiative.** The process is the same as any other encounter, but crew members can choose to roll a skill associated with the battle station they're helming. The player starship doesn't roll its own initiative, as its actions are determined by the crew, but enemy starships and hazards always roll initiative.
2. **Play a round.** Each crew member gets three actions to use on their turn. Turns are typically spent on taking station actions and moving between stations, but if a battle station malfunctions or a boarding pod hits the hull, characters have to make some tough choices about how to use their actions!
3. **Begin the next round.** Combat continues in rounds until the GM determines the encounter has ended—typically when all enemy starships are defeated and all hazards are disabled, or when the player starship has been defeated.
4. **End the encounter.** Crew members earn experience for defeating foes and overcoming hazards just as they would for any other combat encounter. Encounters don't need to continue until every enemy ship is destroyed; a pirate crew might surrender rather than see their ship scrapped, or a military scout might flee after taking heavy damage. If the player starship is defeated instead, the GM might have enemies send boarding parties, starting a new normal combat encounter.

## New Rules
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 170*

While the structure is the same as other combat encounters, tactical starship combat introduces new rules, traits, and conditions.

### New Traits
**Bypassing** An effect with the bypassing trait ignores Shield Points when dealing damage to a starship, instead affecting Hull Points directly.

**Starship** Starships are massive space-worthy objects capable of transporting creatures and cargo across great distances. Creatures with the starship trait are massive creatures that have adapted to survive in space. Actions, spells, or other abilities with the starship trait are intended to be used in tactical starship combat against threats on the sensor map. Creatures and objects with the starship trait can generally only be damaged or otherwise affected by actions, creatures, and items with the starship trait.

### Helming Battle Stations
To helm a battle station, a crew member must occupy its square and must have at least one free hand. A crew member can't helm a battle station if they're unconscious or otherwise incapacitated. A crew member keeps helming the battle station until they're no longer occupying its square. Only one character can helm a battle station at a time.

A character helming a station gains access to its station actions. Battle stations often grant one or two additional effects, such as allowing a character in the pilot's console to use their Piloting DC in place of the starship's AC. Battle stations upgraded to a higher grade often grant additional station actions and benefits.

### Zones
Distance on the sensor map is measured in zones rather than feet or squares. Zones are abstract; they represent approximate and relative positions of starships, hazards, and other objects rather than exact distances. Any number of starships and objects can share the same zone. Zones are primarily used to determine whether starships' attacks and abilities are in range. Starship weapon ranges are described in zones: a weapon with a range of 1 zone can target enemies in its zone and up to 1 zone away. Starships in the same zone are locked in a close-up skirmish, while starships firing at one another from different zones are engaging in a long-range duel.

Whether to maneuver to a different zone is often an important decision; the pilot must consider the crew's capabilities, the enemies' weapon ranges, and the proximity of features that provide cover or hide environmental hazards. Some actions require a target to be in the same zone, and others can be used from great distances or have different effects depending on range. For example, the crew can catch another starship off-guard by getting behind it before firing.

## Movement, Heading and Areas
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 171*

### Starship Movement
Just like most creatures, starships have a Speed. This Speed is measured in zones and determines how far the starship can travel through space. The most typical way of moving a player starship is using the pilot's console to perform the Boost Thrusters action, which can usually be done only once per round.

### Heading
A starship's heading determines the direction it's pointing toward on the sensor map. The GM usually determines the heading of all ships at the beginning of an encounter based on the circumstances; otherwise, the crew member helming the pilot's console determines the player starship's heading at the beginning of combat. A starship changes heading when a crew member helming the pilot's console uses Turn or another station action that changes the starship's heading.

A starship is **off-guard** to attacks made from the opposite direction of its heading. Starships in the same zone always treat each other as being in the direction of their heading. Specific rules and actions might have different results depending on starships' headings.

### Areas on the Sensor Map
Areas of effect on the sensor map are calculated similarly to those in non-starship encounters, adjusted for zones.
- **Burst.** Originates from the line bordering two zones and extends in both directions for a number of zones equal to its value. A 0-zone burst instead affects a single zone in range.
- **Cone.** Starts from its origin (usually your zone) and extends in one direction for a number of zones equal to its value. It affects all targets in each of these zones, including your zone, but doesn't affect the starship it originates from.
- **Emanation.** Extends to the listed distance from your zone; an emanation of 1 zone affects your starship's zone and one zone to either side. An emanation of 0 zones only affects your starship's zone.
- **Line.** Starts from its origin (usually your zone) and extends in one direction for a number of zones equal to its value. A line can target only up to one starship in each zone it occupies, including your zone; you choose which target it affects in each zone.

## Damaging Starships
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 171–172*

A player starship's Hull Points, Shield Points, and saving throws are determined by its frame and level.

### Hull Points
Instead of Hit Points, starships have **Hull Points** (HP), which represent structural integrity. When a starship takes damage, reduce its current Hull Points by the damage dealt. Some station actions, items, and other effects, including downtime repairs, restore Hull Points, up to the starship's maximum. If it's unclear whether an effect referencing HP means Hit Points or Hull Points, check whether the effect has the starship trait or originates from a starship or starship hazard; any such effect typically affects Hull Points.

### Shield Points
A starship's **Shield Points** (SP) represent how much damage its shields can sustain before being depleted. Track current and maximum Shield Points separately from Hull Points; when a starship takes damage, reduce its Shield Points first, then apply any remaining damage to its Hull Points.

Shield Points can't be restored by effects that restore Hull Points, but they can be restored by other means, such as the Generate Shields action at the generator. An effect can restore Shield Points even if the starship has 0 Hull Points; this has no effect on Hull Points or conditions resulting from hull damage, but the Shield Points might still protect the ship from taking enough damage to be destroyed.

### Starship Saving Throws
When the player starship attempts a save, any crew member can choose to roll on behalf of the starship. Some higher-grade battle stations grant an item bonus to one or more of the starship's saving throws as long as a crew member is helming that station—for example, a superior-grade pilot's console grants a +1 item bonus to Reflex saves, regardless of which crew member rolls the save. A crew member rolling the save can spend one of their Hero Points to reroll, as the starship doesn't have its own Hero Points. Enemy and NPC starships roll saving throws much like monsters do.

### Starship Immunities
Most starships are objects that are immune to bleed, death effects, disease, drained, fatigued, paralyzed, poison, sickened, spirit, vitality, and void. Starships can never become doomed or unconscious. Unlike other objects, most starships aren't immune to mental effects, since such effects have an impact on the crew. Living starships often have different immunities, as detailed in their stat blocks. Starships aren't immune to nonlethal attacks or effects by default and can be incapacitated by them, assuming they come from a source that can damage the starship in the first place.

### Starships Against Creatures
Starships, including their weapons and defenses, operate on a different scale than PCs and most creatures. If creatures shoot at a starship with normal munitions, cast spells at it, or take other hostile actions while it's on the ground or within range, its shields repel the damage. The effects of starship weapons against creatures are especially devastating; when there's a chance targets might survive, they're typically simulated with a hazard or chase scene.

## Destroying Starships
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 172–173*

The rules for destroying starships function in many ways like the death and dying rules, with key differences. When a player starship is reduced to 0 Hull Points, it first becomes **compromised** (rather than dying), and its battle stations become **malfunctioning**. If the starship escapes destruction, usually by regaining Hull Points, it's **wrecked** (rather than wounded) until sufficiently repaired.

### Becoming Compromised
Starships can't be reduced to fewer than 0 Hull Points. When most non-player starships reach 0 Hull Points, they're destroyed and removed from play. Player starships aren't automatically destroyed; instead, when a player starship is reduced to 0 Hull Points:
- The starship gains the compromised 1 condition, or compromised 2 if the effect was a critical success from the attacker or the result of the starship's critical failure. If the starship is wrecked, increase its compromised value by its wrecked value. If the damage was nonlethal, the ship doesn't become compromised; it's instead **inoperable** with 0 Hull Points.
- Compromised always includes a value; if it reaches compromised 10, the ship is destroyed. The value changes through hull integrity checks, taking damage, and ship repairs.
- Each battle station gains the malfunctioning condition. This prevents most station actions, though crew members can still move around the ship, fight boarders, gain passive benefits from helming stations, and—most importantly—conduct repairs to restore function to battle stations and stop the ship from being destroyed!
- Note the initiative count when the starship became compromised. The starship enters initiative directly before the turn in which it was reduced to 0 HP. This is when it attempts hull integrity checks in subsequent rounds. It leaves initiative if it loses the compromised condition.

### Hull Integrity Checks
While a starship is compromised, it must attempt a hull integrity check each round on its turn. Any crew member can attempt this check; in a tie with other characters and starships on that initiative, it goes first. This is a flat check with a DC equal to 10 + the ship's current compromised value.

**Critical Success** The compromised value is reduced by 2.

**Success** The compromised value is reduced by 1.

**Failure** The compromised value increases by 1.

**Critical Failure** The compromised value increases by 2.

### Taking Damage While Compromised
If a compromised starship takes damage that isn't completely mitigated (by Shield Points, for example), increase its compromised value by 1, or by 2 if the damage came from an attacker's critical hit or the starship's own critical failure at a save. Additionally, each battle station gains the malfunctioning condition.

### Ship Repairs
A starship doesn't regain Hull Points automatically and must be actively repaired. In combat, Hull Points can be restored with certain spells (such as cosmic repairs), station actions (such as Jump Start), or actions granted by upgrades and drones (such as Repair Hull). Outside of combat, repairs are typically handled with the **Emergency Repairs** exploration activity, which restores Hull Points and is the primary method of decreasing a starship's wrecked value.

### Losing the Compromised Condition
A starship loses the compromised condition if it reaches compromised 0 or if it regains 1 Hull Point or more. Any time a starship loses the compromised condition, it gains wrecked 1, or increases its wrecked value by 1. Restoring Hull Points or removing compromised doesn't automatically remove the malfunctioning condition from battle stations, though many actions that restore Hull Points, such as Jump Start, also do so.

### Destruction
A destroyed starship becomes a ruin floating aimlessly in space. Its battle stations stop working and can't be repaired. The interior has zero gravity, and creatures on board need environmental protections or other means of life support to breathe. The starship can't regain Hull Points unless it's returned to a port or way station for repairs. Crew members on a destroyed starship are stranded unless they installed escape pods or possess another means of escape. In some adventures, an enemy starship might explode in a devastating blast that leaves no surviving crew.

## Modes of Play and Starships
*Starfinder Tech Core, p. 172, 174*

While you're on your starship, you can engage with each mode of play in a unique way.
- **Encounter mode.** Characters interact with a map of their starship as well as the enemies and hazards on the sensor map. Characters can use most of their abilities as normal, but they can't affect starship-scale objects and creatures unless they're helming a starship station.
- **Exploration mode.** Players choose starship exploration activities to represent their activities while traveling. These activities also determine what they're doing at the start of any tactical starship encounter that might arise.
- **Downtime mode.** Takes place over days, often during travel through the Drift, so PCs might be limited in the downtime activities they can choose. New activities are available on starships with expansion bays.

### Starship Exploration Activities
Exploration mode occurs when a crew is traveling somewhere or investigating something on board a starship. Tell the GM what you'd generally like to do on the way—it isn't necessary to go into extreme detail; "I'm scanning for enemies" is sufficient. These activities often determine both where you are on the ship and what you can do if tactical starship combat breaks out. Many of them are determined by the battle stations on your starship. ## In This Module
How the rules above are handled by *SF2e Tactical Starship Combat*.
- **Initiative.** Every PC keeps their own initiative and turn. The player starship has no turn of its own; it enters initiative only while compromised, directly before the turn in which it dropped to 0 HP, for its hull integrity check.
- **Battle stations.** Claim a station on the ship sheet, in the Crew Manager, or by moving onto its square. Station actions appear on your character sheet during the encounter and in the Station HUD.
- **Sensor Map.** Zones, headings, ranges, burst / cone / emanation / line previews and detection (undetected, hidden, observed, identified) are computed automatically. Ships are moved by dragging.
- **Damage.** Immunities, weaknesses, resistances and hardness apply first, then Shield Points (except bypassing damage), then Hull Points. Compromised, wrecked, inoperable and destroyed are tracked; hull integrity checks are rolled from the ship sheet.
